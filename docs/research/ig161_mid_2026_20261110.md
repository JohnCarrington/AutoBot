# AutoBot 161's 2026 mid candles turned into IG buy and sell prices

10 November 2026. Research only: nothing here changes live trading. It follows `dukascopy_check_20261109.md`. Dukascopy was dropped on the owner's instruction (10 November).

## Summary

- **The method:** sell = mid − half the spread, buy = mid + half, for each of open, high, low and close. The spread is **IG's typical spread for that half hour of the week**, measured from the bot's own IG prices. Each price is rounded to IG's tenth of a point, sell down and buy up, so the spread is never narrower than the one used.
- **161's mid is exactly the bot's IG price.** For the 2,826 5-minute candles both hold (25 October to 6 November), 161's mid equals the bot's (buy + sell) / 2 on **11,298 of 11,304 prices**. The other 6 are two rollover candles on 27 October, at most 0.7 apart. So the only unknown in the conversion is the spread.
- **The check, 25 October to 6 November, against the bot's real IG candles:**

  | Hours | Exact | Typical | 95% within | Largest |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | Normal hours (all but the three rows below) | about 58% | 0.0 | 0.3 points | 7.4 to 7.5 (news spikes the table cannot know) |
  | Rollover hour, 22:00 to 23:00 UK | 24–26% | 0.5–1.1 | 5.2–6.8 | 6.9–7.6 |
  | Sunday's first hour, 21:00 to 22:00 UK | about 1% | about 3.0 | about 4 | — |

  Bigger candles built from the rebuilt 5-minute ones: hourly typically 0.0 apart (95% within about 2.5 to 3.4), daily within 0.3 except one close of 3.2.
- **Two problems in 161's files, found and handled as the owner decided:**
  1. **January and February are five hours early** (New York time written as UTC). They are moved 5 hours later and kept, but they only roughly match IG: a median of 0.5 points, not exact.
  2. **161's spread folder (`ohlc`) is not usable as IG's spread.** Its prices track IG only roughly (never exactly; 29 points off in April), and its spreads are about a quarter of IG's at the rollover. **The half-hour table is used for the whole of 2026 instead** (owner's decision).
- **Result:** `data/research/candles_ig161_2026/GBPUSD/` holds **56,468 5-minute, 4,738 hourly and 237 daily** buy and sell candles, from 1 January to 6 November 2026. That is 88% of the 5-minute candles IG's hours expect; the rest is 161's own missing time, all recorded in `gaps.csv`. The files are in the bot's store format and read in the live `CandleStore`'s own reader. No buy price is below its sell.

## What was and was not touched

- No IG calls, no restart of the price service. Nothing was written outside `data/research/`; the code refuses to (tested). The bot's own candles were only read.
- Long jobs ran at `nice -n 19 ionice -c3` (the full conversion took 16 seconds).
- Committed: this report, `research/mid_to_bidask.py`, its tests in `tests/technical/test_research_candles.py`, and a note in `dukascopy_check_20261109.md` that Dukascopy was dropped. No data is committed.

## 2. The check: 25 October to 6 November against the bot's real IG candles

All 2,826 of 161's mid candles in the window are also in the bot's store. Each was turned into buy and sell, and all 8 prices were compared with the bot's real IG candle. Distances are rebuilt minus real, in points.

**Two spread tables were tried.** The table in `dukascopy_check_20261109.md` was measured on 25 October to 10 November, so it has seen the very prices it is tested on (in-sample). To see how it does on days it has not seen, a second table was built only from IG's hourly candles of 7 August to 23 October (out-of-sample; spreads from each hour's first and last moments only).

**Rounding** (in-sample table, all hours): sell down and buy up gave 57% exact, 95% within 0.5. Sell up and buy down: 57%, 0.4. No rounding (twentieths IG never writes): 56%, 0.45. The rounding choice hardly matters, so the conversion keeps sell down and buy up, which never understates the cost.

**Reading it:**

- **In normal hours the rebuilt prices are as good as real.** More than half are exact, 95% are within 0.3 points, and both tables agree. The occasional larger miss is a short spread spike (news) that no typical-spread table can know.
- **The rollover is the weak spot.** IG's spread there moves between about 2 and 25 within the hour, so a single typical figure is often 1 to 5 points off for a given candle. On average the rebuilt prices come out a little wide (the buy too high and the sell too low by 0.85 in-sample, 1.76 out-of-sample), which errs on the side of cost. Replays should not rely on exact fills in the 22:00 hour. The bot already opens no slow grind trades from 21:55 to 22:15.
- **Sunday's first hour** is typically 3 points off in either table: the opening spread swings widely from week to week.
- **The out-of-sample table is worse at 23:00** (typical 0.8, average +3.4), because an hourly candle's opening moment at 23:00 still sits in the rollover's tail. The 5-minute-based table does not have that problem. It should be rebuilt as the bot's store grows, to stop depending on the same days it was tested on.

## 3. Why 161's spread folder was not used

The instruction was to use 161's stored IG spreads from its `ohlc` folder for 1 January to 10 April. The data shows they are not the spread the bot pays:

- **Its prices are not exactly IG's, nor exactly 161's mid candles.** Against 161's mid (January and February corrected) no bar has all four prices equal; the largest leg is typically 0.4 points apart in January and February, 0.85 in March, and **29 in April**. Against IG's own daily candles it is 0.3 to 0.5 points off in January to March and **16 to 27 points off in April**. 161 no longer has the code that wrote the folder.
- **Its spreads do not look like IG's spread bet:**

  | | 161's `spread_avg` (Jan–Apr) | Bot's real IG spread (Aug–Nov) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Daytime, 07:00–21:00 UK | 0.61–0.66 | 0.9 (or 1.5) |
  | Overnight, 00:00–07:00 UK | about 0.75 | 0.9 (summer) / 1.5 (November) |
  | Rollover, 22:00 UK | **4.2** | **16 to 17** |
  | Spread 0.0 | 2,108 bars (all completely flat) | never |
  | Typical peaks (`spread_max`) | 1.0, 1.1, 1.2 | 0.9, 1.5 |

  Using these would have made 2026's first 14 weeks look about a third cheaper in the day and about four times cheaper at the rollover. The likeliest explanation is a different IG price feed (perhaps the CFD account's), but that is not proven.

On the owner's decision, **the half-hour table is used for the whole of 2026**. The spread folder stays in `data/research/ig161/GBPUSD_ohlc_2026/` as received.

## 4. The conversion

```
nice -n 19 ionice -c3 .venv/bin/python -m research.mid_to_bidask \
    --src data/research/ig161/GBPUSD_mid_2026 \
    --table data/research/ig_spread/GBPUSD_spread_by_half_hour.csv \
    --out data/research/candles_ig161_2026/GBPUSD
```

1. Read 161's mid candles: snap each price to the twentieth, skip the 2 `nan` rows, and move January and February 5 hours later.
2. Keep only candles inside IG's market hours, using the bot's own settings. 10 were left out: 5 on Saturday evenings and 3 on Sundays before IG's 21:00 open (all in April), and 2 just after a Friday close (17 April 22:05, 1 May 22:00).
3. For each 5-minute candle, look up IG's typical spread for its UK half hour of the week. In the weeks when US and UK clocks are out of step, weekdays use the row one hour later, because IG's rollover follows New York time. Then sell = mid − half, buy = mid + half, rounded sell down and buy up.
4. Build hourly and daily candles exactly as the bot does (`research.candles_convert`: UK midnight days, IG's short Sunday candle, buy and sell separately, `parts` and `expected_parts`), write them in the store's format, and record every gap.

**Gaps** (`data/research/candles_ig161_2026/GBPUSD/gaps.csv`; 7,613 5-minute candles in 138 runs):

| Where | Runs | 5-minute candles |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Whole weeks missing: 1 to 20 March** (161 has nothing) | 3 | 4,332 |
| IG's first hour (Sunday 21:00 to 22:00 UK) missing: the 8 January–February Sundays, 12 April, 14 June, 18 October; part of it on 5 and 19 April | 13 | 144 |
| 161 starting late after a weekend outage: 22 and 29 March, 17 and 24 May, 7 June, 12 and 19 July, 20 September, 11 October (12 July: until Tuesday 14:10) | 9 | 1,647 |
| Other outages of an hour or more (for example 1 to 2 April, 8 to 10 April, 15 April, 14 to 15 May, 10 July, 22 to 23 July, 18 September) | 14 | 1,314 |
| Short gaps under an hour, mostly around the rollover | 99 | 176 |

By month, 161 is complete only in parts. March holds just 23 to 31 March, and April and May have several day-long outages. Replays should read `parts` / `expected_parts` and `gaps.csv` before trusting a day.
